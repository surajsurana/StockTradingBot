## Why It Failed the Real Test

The overall backtest metrics look attractive — positive expectancy (₹125.89), profit factor 1.24, Sharpe 0.91 — but these are aggregate/in-sample figures. The Auditor's rejection hinges on the out-of-sample expectancy of -₹162.15 per trade, meaning that on data the strategy wasn't tuned against, the edge inverts. This is the classic signature of a Gap-and-Go setup that curve-fit to specific historical gap sizes, VWAP-hold thresholds, or volatility regimes present in the training window but not persistent going forward.

The concentration numbers explain *how* the in-sample edge was manufactured. Nearly all profit came from the 9 AM entry bucket (₹14,274 of ₹18,632 total), with 10 AM contributing a smaller share and 12 PM entries actively losing money — suggesting the mechanism (gap continuation confirmed by VWAP hold) only works in the narrow opening-hour window and decays fast after. Similarly, PnL was almost entirely a bearish-regime phenomenon (+₹21,897 bearish vs. -₹3,265 bullish), implying the "hold above VWAP" logic is really capturing short-covering/gap-down-reversal dynamics rather than a generalizable gap-and-go continuation pattern. Sector dispersion (IT/Financials strongly positive, Metals/Chemicals/FMCG strongly negative) further suggests the strategy is riding a handful of liquid, low-slippage names rather than a universal microstructure edge.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Retest with entries restricted to 9:00–9:30 AM and bearish-regime days only, using a fresh out-of-sample year to see if that narrower rule still fails. (2) Build a sector-conditioned filter or exclude commodity-sensitive sectors (Metals, Chemicals, Oil & Gas) and re-run walk-forward validation to check if the negative OOS expectancy was concentrated there.
