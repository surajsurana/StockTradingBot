## Why the End-of-Day Reversal hypothesis fails

The mechanism assumes that intraday moves overextend and mean-revert into the close. But the numbers show a systematic, one-directional bleed rather than noise around a true edge: win rate of 29.2%, profit factor 0.34 (losses are ~3x the size of wins), and expectancy of -24.08/trade in-sample, deteriorating to -24.14 out-of-sample. That consistency across in-sample and OOS is itself informative — this isn't a lucky-window artifact, it's a structurally negative mechanism. If EOD reversal worked, you'd expect asymmetric payoff (small frequent losses, occasional large reversal wins); instead the strategy is losing on both frequency and magnitude.

The regime and time data confirm the mechanism is broken, not just mistimed: 100% of trades fired in the same entry-hour bucket (14:00) and 100% of P&L came from bearish-Nifty days (-24,270.98), with zero trades tagged bullish/unknown. This means the "reversal" signal is really just a proxy for shorting into pre-existing bearish momentum at 2 PM — likely continuation, not reversal, in the underlying regime tested. Sector losses are broad-based but concentrated in Financial Services (-10,590, ~44% of total P&L) and IT/FMCG, suggesting index-heavy, high-liquidity names trended rather than reverted.

Follow-up ideas: (1) test the same 14:00 setup as a momentum/continuation strategy instead of reversal, isolating bearish-regime days; (2) diversify entry-hour buckets to see if reversal has any validity earlier in the session, since single-hour, single-regime testing may have masked heterogeneous behavior.
