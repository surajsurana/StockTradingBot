# Analysis: Breadth-Thrust Laggard Catch-Up — REJECT

The overall metrics look deceptively survivable in-sample (positive expectancy of 3.08, PF 1.01, CAGR 0.19%), but this is a razor-thin edge sitting almost exactly at breakeven, and it collapses to -43.62 expectancy out-of-sample. A strategy this fragile in-sample was never robust enough to expect stability out-of-sample — PF of 1.01 means essentially every winning trade is offset by a losing one, so any regime shift in the OOS window flips the sign.

The mechanism's dependency structure confirms this fragility. The entire P&L is concentrated in the 10:00 hour, which alone lost -115,650 — likely breadth-thrust signals firing on noisy opening reversals rather than genuine laggard catch-up. Later hours (12:00-14:00) were the actual profit engine, suggesting the "thrust" signal needs more confirmation time before laggards move, not an immediate entry. Regime dependence is stark: -130,482 in bullish tape vs +148,695 in bearish tape — the strategy is really a bearish-breadth mean-reversion play mislabeled as a general breadth-thrust strategy, and it only works when broad market weakness forces laggards to catch up on relief bounces. Sector-wise, IT/Financials/Construction Materials drove gains while Metals & Mining, Capital Goods, and FMCG bled heavily, indicating no genuine cross-sector breadth effect, just a few idiosyncratic clusters.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Isolate and retest purely as a bearish-regime, post-10:30 entry strategy with an explicit market-regime filter. (2) Investigate why Metals/Capital Goods laggards never catch up — possibly structural short-only sectors — and exclude them rather than assuming universal breadth applicability.
