## Why the Verdict Makes Sense

End-of-Day Reversal bets that late-session moves snap back the next session. The numbers show the opposite is happening systematically: a 33.6% win rate paired with a 0.46 profit factor means losers are both more frequent and larger than winners -- consistent with the strategy fading moves that are actually continuations, not reversals, in the current regime. With all 9,312 trades entered in the single 14:00 bucket, this is a concentrated, undiversified-by-time bet, so there's no intraday averaging effect to smooth out a bad mechanism -- every trade shares the same structural flaw.

The regime split is the clearest tell: bearish-day trades lost -188,290 versus -50,239 on bullish days. Reversal-fading strategies typically bleed hardest in trending/bearish tapes because "reversals" are shallower and continuation dominates -- exactly what's showing up here. Sector losses are broad-based (17 of 18 sectors negative), with Financial Services (-57k), Metals & Mining (-28.9k), and IT (-19.9k) the largest bleeders -- these are high-beta, high-volume sectors where trend persistence on bearish days would punish a reversal entry hardest. This isn't a sector-specific failure; it's a mechanism failure that shows up everywhere, just scaled by liquidity/volatility.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test whether restricting entries to bullish-regime days only still fails, to isolate whether the edge is regime-conditional rather than universal. (2) Given uniform failure across sectors/regimes, don't tune reversal parameters (SEED-ORB-2 lesson) -- instead test a continuation/momentum variant at the same 14:00 entry time, since the data suggests trend-following, not fading, is what's actually happening late-session.
