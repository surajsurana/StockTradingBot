# Analysis: Cross-Sectional Momentum — PASS

**Mechanism and why it worked:** The strategy's edge is asymmetric — a 30.6% win rate paired with a 1.78 profit factor and +699.98 expectancy means winners run far longer than losers (consistent with the 126-day time-stop letting momentum trends play out while the 8% synthetic stop caps downside). This is the classic momentum signature: few big winners, many small stopped-out losses. The 91-trade / 42-holdout split with 100% positive-expectancy windows suggests the edge is structurally stable across time, not a lucky vintage — though with single-vintage holding (one entry path per episode, no overlapping-portfolio smoothing), realized variance is materially higher than the paper's factor-return construction, so month-to-month results are lumpy (e.g., +26.7% in Jul-2021 vs -9.4% in May-2022).

**Dependencies:** P&L is heavily concentrated in Capital Goods, Healthcare, Chemicals, and Financial Services — cyclical/trending sectors well-suited to 6-month formation momentum — while Auto, Oil & Gas, and Metals & Mining were net drags, likely mean-reverting or macro-driven sectors where momentum breaks down. Critically, 84% of total P&L (64,728 of 77,699) came from bearish-regime entries — odd for a long-only momentum strategy, implying the model captures relative-strength leaders holding up during broader Nifty weakness rather than pure beta-chasing.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test a skip-period (J=6/K=6 with 1-month gap) to check if the bearish-regime dependency is partly short-term reversal contamination. (2) Isolate Auto/Oil&Gas/Metals exclusion or sector-neutral construction to see if removing structural laggards improves Sharpe beyond 0.931.
