# Why the MAX Effect Passed — and What Drives It

The lottery-demand anomaly (long-only, MAX(1) skewness-chasing) shows a marginal but real edge: 50.45% win rate, 1.21 profit factor, +87.09 expectancy across 335 trades, and a positive-expectancy OOS holdout (+73.79/trade, n=120). This is consistent with the mechanism — investors overpay for stocks with recent extreme positive returns (lottery tickets), and this strategy captures the reversion/premium associated with *not* chasing that overpricing, or rides momentum in the right regime — but the effect is thin and regime-dependent, not robust across all conditions.

**Regime dependency is the dominant story**: P&L by market regime shows +30,308 in bullish Nifty conditions versus -1,132 in bearish — essentially all profit comes from bull tape. This is corroborated by the annual returns: 2024 (+36.06%) was a strong bull year for the strategy, while 2025 (-14.99%) coincided with a drawdown-heavy stretch (max DD 25.41%, rolling annual return falling to -15.47% by Oct 2025). The strategy is not sector-neutral either — Financial Services, FMCG, and Healthcare drove the bulk of gains (~27.7k combined), while Construction, Media, and Capital Goods bled money (-11.9k combined), suggesting the MAX effect interacts with sector-specific volatility/liquidity characteristics rather than being a uniform cross-sectional signal.

**Follow-ups**: (1) Condition entries on a bullish-regime filter given the stark bullish/bearish P&L asymmetry — this alone may materially improve Sharpe (currently 0.707) without touching trade count. (2) Test the paper's overlapping-portfolio construction against this single-vintage build, since that's flagged as moderate-to-material and directionally unknown — it could explain part of the sector concentration and drawdown severity.
