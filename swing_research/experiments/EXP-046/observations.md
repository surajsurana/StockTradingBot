# Analysis: Idiosyncratic Volatility Anomaly — REJECT

**Why the verdict holds despite decent-looking aggregate numbers:** The overall book shows a positive expectancy (+81.02), profit factor 1.19, and modest CAGR (8.56%), but the auditor's rejection rests on the out-of-sample split showing -77.15 expectancy — a sign+magnitude reversal from the full-sample figure. This is consistent with the disclosed fidelity gaps: substituting single-factor market-model residuals for the paper's 3-factor (market+SMB+HML) construction likely misclassifies "idiosyncratic" volatility on NSE, since size and value effects are material in this cross-section but are now absorbed into the residual rather than stripped out. The result is a long-only low-idio-vol leg that captured cheap in-sample regime tailwinds (2024 CAGR 19.25%, rolling-annual as high as 42% in late 2024) but degraded sharply as the sample rolled forward — 2026 annual return is -10.53%, with March 2026 alone at -18.26%, and rolling-annual return turns negative by April 2026. That collapse in the most recent, least-overlapped-with-training window is exactly what an OOS expectancy check is designed to catch.

**Dependencies:** Sector P&L is highly concentrated — Healthcare, Auto, Capital Goods, Financials drove nearly all gains, while Chemicals (-7,454) and Consumer Services (-9,944) were persistent drags, suggesting the "anomaly" is really a sector-timing artifact rather than a clean idio-vol effect. Regime split (bullish 14,709 vs bearish 13,243) shows no real edge concentration — performance is regime-agnostic, which itself is odd for a vol-based signal and hints the single-factor residual is picking up something closer to a sector/reversal blend.
