# Analysis: Idiosyncratic Volatility Anomaly — PASS

**Why it performed this way:** The strategy earned a marginal but real edge (PF 1.11, expectancy +43.03) with weak risk-adjusted quality (Sharpe 0.405, max drawdown 23.06% against a 24.62% total return on capital — a poor recovery factor of 0.718). This profile is consistent with a low-idio-vol long-only selection leg riding market beta rather than capturing a clean idiosyncratic-vol premium: the strategy made 17,927 in bearish-regime entries vs. only 6,688 in bullish ones, suggesting the edge is concentrated in defensive/low-vol stocks outperforming during down-market stress — a beta-driven, not purely idiosyncratic, effect. This is exactly what you'd expect from a single-factor (CAPM) residual substituting for the paper's 3-factor construction: without SMB/HML stripped out, size and value effects likely leak into the "idiosyncratic" residual, inflating apparent stock-picking skill. Annual returns swinging from -9.35% (2021) to +24.64% (2023) to -10.6% (2026) with no stable trend further points to regime-dependent, not structural, alpha.

**Sector dependency:** Gains were concentrated in Auto/Auto Components (+18.4k) and Capital Goods (+10.8k) — cyclical/value-tilted sectors — while Chemicals (-11.4k), Consumer Services (-7.5k), and Consumer Durables (-4.9k) bled heavily, suggesting sector concentration risk rather than a diversified factor premium.
