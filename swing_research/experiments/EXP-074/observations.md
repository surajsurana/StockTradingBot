# Analysis: Idiosyncratic Volatility Anomaly (PASS)

**Why it likely worked this way:** The strategy captures only the long, low-idio-vol stock-selection leg of a single-factor (CAPM residual) construction rather than the paper's 3-factor long-short spread — a real edge in low-idiosyncratic-risk names, but a narrower and less-purified version of the published anomaly. This shows up in the metrics: a modest 50.2% win rate paired with a 1.27 profit factor and +235.78 expectancy suggests the edge comes from a favorable payoff skew (winners outrunning losers) rather than high hit rate — consistent with a factor tilt rather than a timing signal. The 39.6% max drawdown against a 0.92 Sharpe indicates the return stream is lumpy and regime-sensitive, not smooth compounding.

**Dependencies:** P&L is heavily concentrated in FMCG (+83.8k), IT (+62.0k), and Financials (+37.1k) — traditionally lower-beta, stable-earnings sectors, which is exactly where low-idio-vol names cluster. Conversely, Consumer Services (-43.2k) and Media (-20.0k) dragged results, likely higher-volatility/speculative names misclassified as "low idio-vol" under the cruder single-factor residual (no SMB/HML control lets size/value effects contaminate the sort). Regime split (bullish +187.8k vs bearish +73.7k) shows the strategy is long-only beta-dependent — it still needs up-days to fire, undermining the "market-neutral" framing of the original paper.
