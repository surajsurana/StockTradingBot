# Analysis: Downside Beta (Long High-Quintile) — PASS

**Mechanism fit.** This strategy longs the highest-downside-beta quintile — stocks that fall hardest when the market falls, which the academic literature says should carry an *equity premium* for bearing that crash risk. The regime breakdown confirms the mechanism is working roughly as expected in reverse of what a naive reading suggests: PnL is concentrated in **bearish-entry-day trades (+19,579)** while bullish-entry-day trades are net negative (-4,809). High-downside-beta names appear to be mean-reverting or getting bought aggressively after bearish days in this sample, which is plausible given the 8% stop and 1%-risk sizing — the stop caps the "gap risk" tail that a high-beta long is exposed to on continuation days, letting the bounce trades carry the book.

**Sector dependence.** Returns are highly concentrated, not diversified: Construction (+8,329), Power (+4,177), Diversified (+3,930), Media (+3,732) and Financial Services (+3,081) supply almost all the edge, while Telecom (-5,445), Oil & Gas (-2,377) and IT (-1,965) are structural drags. With only 10 positions drawn from a ~90-name quintile, sector concentration is a sizing artifact (disclosed as "moderate, changes dispersion") rather than evidence the factor is sector-neutral — a different draw of 10 names could easily flip the sign.

**Follow-ups.** (1) Test sector-neutral capping or exclude Telecom/Oil&Gas/IT to see if expectancy survives without those drags — this tells us if the edge is genuinely cross-sectional or sector-driven. (2) Since bearish-day entries dominate, explicitly condition entry on prior-day Nifty direction and compare bull vs bear sub-books' Sharpe/Sortino separately, given the aggregate Sharpe (0.369) is anemic and may be hiding a much stronger bear-regime-only signal diluted by weak bull-regime trades.
