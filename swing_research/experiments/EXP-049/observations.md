**Why it passed:** The turn-of-month effect tested here is a per-symbol reinterpretation of an index-level phenomenon, applied uniformly to an undifferentiated universe rather than a curated cross-section. With the per-month combined-rank diversification fix (40 eligible names/month, genuinely reshuffled monthly), the strategy achieved decent breadth — a 68.82 expectancy, 1.46 profit factor, and a positive-expectancy holdout (137.90/trade over 105 trades) that cleared the bar despite only 50% of walk-forward windows being positive. That 50% figure and the choppy monthly return pattern (2024 was a -6.35% year with five straight losing months from May–Aug 2024) suggest the effect is real but weak and regime-dependent, not a robust daily edge — consistent with a calendar anomaly that shows up in aggregate but is noisy stock-by-stock.

**Dependencies:** P&L is heavily concentrated in Financial Services (+7,672) and Consumer Durables (+4,137), which together account for more than half the total P&L; Telecom, Chemicals, Oil & Gas, Diversified, and IT were net losers. The bullish/bearish split (11,599 vs 9,185) shows the edge persists in both regimes but is meaningfully stronger when Nifty is bullish on entry day — turn-of-month effects are known to partly proxy for institutional flow/rebalancing, which is dampened in bearish tapes.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test the original index-level signal directly (e.g., trade a Nifty proxy or ETF on the same calendar day) to isolate whether the edge is genuinely a market-wide phenomenon or an artifact of stock-level noise. (2) Re-run with sector-neutral or capped sector exposure, since Financial Services/Consumer Durables dominance may be a hidden sector-timing effect wearing a calendar-effect label.
