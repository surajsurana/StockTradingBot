# Analysis: MAX Effect (Lottery-Demand Anomaly) — PASS

**Mechanism and why it plausibly worked:** The MAX effect posits that stocks with high recent maximum daily returns get bid up by lottery-seeking demand, then mean-revert — this is a short-side anomaly at its core (overpriced lottery stocks underperform). Given the LONG-ONLY constraint, this strategy can only harvest the anomaly indirectly (e.g., via momentum-adjacent effects in MAX(1) stocks that continue drifting, or by avoiding the worst decile). That the strategy still cleared a positive-expectancy bar (94.33/trade, PF 1.25) despite fighting its own long-only handicap suggests the long leg captures a real, if diluted, signal — but the modest Sharpe (0.794) and heavy max drawdown (23.48%) versus recovery factor near 1.0 show this edge is thin and fragile, consistent with a strategy shorn of its natural short leg.

**Dependencies:** P&L is heavily concentrated in Healthcare (+16.4k) and FMCG (+15.9k) — together exceeding total P&L of 32k — while Chemicals (-4.6k) and Consumer Services (-5.6k) were persistent drags. This is not a broad-based effect; it's a two-sector trade wearing a factor-strategy costume. Regime-wise, bullish Nifty days contributed 20.3k vs. 11.7k bearish — the edge tilts toward up-markets, consistent with a long-only lottery-momentum residual that needs risk appetite to work. The 2025 annual return (-12.15%) versus 2024 (+18.88%) and 2026 (+19.20%) confirms strong regime/year dependency, not stationarity.
