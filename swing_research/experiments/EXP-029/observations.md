**Why it likely passed (marginally):** The Amihud premium bets on compensation for holding hard-to-trade names, so it mechanically selects the top illiquidity decile — exactly the population where execution-realism costs (ADV caps, ILLIQ-derived slippage, next-day-open fills) bite hardest. That the strategy still cleared a positive-expectancy bar (PF 1.30, expectancy ₹195/trade, Sharpe 0.44) after these realistic frictions is notable, but the thin margin (Sortino 0.29, max DD 25.3%, recovery factor 1.36) suggests the raw zero-cost edge was substantially larger and much of it was consumed by slippage/sizing constraints, consistent with the disclosed expectation that this effect would be "at least as large, plausibly larger" than in prior strategies.

**Dependencies:** Performance is concentrated and regime-dependent. Bullish-day entries drove ~81% of P&L (₹42.8k vs ₹9.7k bearish), and returns are lumpy — 2023 (+43%) and 2026 (+24.6%) dominate, while 2018, 2019, 2020, and 2025 were all negative years. Sector-wise, Capital Goods alone contributed over half of total P&L (₹27.5k), with Financial Services and Healthcare next; Telecommunication (-₹8.2k) and Automobile (-₹4.8k) were persistent drags. This is not a broad-based illiquidity effect — it's a few sector/regime windows carrying the book.

(2) Decompose bullish vs. bearish regime slippage separately — since ADV caps bind hardest on illiquid names during selloffs, a regime-conditioned position-sizing rule may be worth its own hypothesis test.
