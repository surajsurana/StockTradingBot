# Analysis: MAX Effect (Lottery-Demand Anomaly) — REJECT

**Why the verdict follows from the numbers:** With only a single walk-forward window (0/1 positive), there's no basis to distinguish a genuine lottery-demand premium from noise. The aggregate stats confirm the fragility: a profit factor of 1.02 is essentially breakeven, Sharpe of 0.097 and Sortino of 0.119 are near-zero risk-adjusted signal, and recovery factor of 0.069 shows the strategy barely earns back its 12.85% drawdown. The 38.9% win rate combined with positive expectancy (₹10.13/trade) implies the edge — if real — lives entirely in tail trades, which is precisely the pattern that makes single-window validation untrustworthy (same failure mode as SEED-ORB-2).

**Regime/sector dependency:** The strategy's entire P&L (+₹12,881 bearish vs. -₹11,970 bullish) came from bearish-Nifty-day entries — for a long-only lottery-demand strategy, this is backwards from the retail-chasing mechanism the MAX effect is supposed to capture (overpaying for upside skew typically clusters in bullish/euphoric tape). This inversion suggests the "edge" is regime-contamination, not the anomaly itself. Sector P&L is equally lopsided: Capital Goods, FMCG, and Financial Services carried the book while Construction Materials (-₹5,681) and Consumer Services (-₹3,937) alone erased most gains — concentration risk, not a broad-based factor.

**Follow-up ideas:** (1) Re-run with overlapping-portfolio construction (the paper's actual design) before fully discarding — single-vintage holding is a MODERATE/MATERIAL disclosed deviation that could be masking a real signal. (2) If revisited, condition explicitly on Nifty regime and exclude Construction/Consumer Services/Realty to test whether a cleaner subset shows multi-window consistency.
