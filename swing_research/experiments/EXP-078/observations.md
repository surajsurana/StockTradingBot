# Analysis: Overnight Return Anomaly (PASS)

**Mechanism fit.** With the 2026-09-06 fix, this backtest now isolates exactly what the overnight-anomaly literature tests: buy at Close(t), sell at Open(t+1). That's a clean, minimal-contamination measurement — a big reason this PASSes where EXP-076 (contaminated by the extra intraday session under `next_day_open`) was REJECTed. The 69.9% win rate and 2.64 profit factor over a genuinely single-session round trip is consistent with a real, if modest-per-trade (₹99.36 expectancy), structural overnight drift rather than an intraday-momentum artifact.

**Dependencies.** PnL is heavily regime-dependent: bullish-day entries generated ₹892.7k vs. ₹281.9k bearish (~76/24 split), so the edge is real but amplified by, not independent of, broad market direction — note the two sharp regime-stress months (2020-03 COVID crash: -9.9%; 2025-04: -23.3%) show the strategy can still bleed hard intraday-to-next-open during dislocations, and the 8% stop-loss (a bolt-on, not part of the original methodology) is doing real work capping tail losses there. Sectorally, it's top-heavy: Capital Goods (₹243.6k) and Financial Services (₹185k) contribute ~36% of total PnL combined, while Diversified/Media/Construction Materials are marginal — the anomaly is not sector-uniform and may be proxying for specific liquidity/ownership structures in those two sectors.

**Follow-ups.** (1) Test regime-conditioning explicitly — a bearish-day filter or reduced sizing on bearish entries to see if the 24% bearish contribution is worth the drawdown risk it carries. (2) Re-run sector-stratified holdout tests isolating Capital Goods/Financials vs. the rest, since aggregate walk-forward PASS may mask concentration risk in two sectors.
