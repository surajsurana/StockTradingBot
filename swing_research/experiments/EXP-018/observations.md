# Analysis: Cross-Sectional Momentum — REJECT

The in-sample/walk-forward picture looks superficially strong: 224 trades, PF 1.80, Sharpe 0.97, Sortino 2.75, CAGR 23.6%, expectancy +1357.92 per trade. But the auditor's verdict rests on the one number that matters most — **out-of-sample expectancy of -54.02** — meaning the edge the backtest found does not survive on data the model never touched. This is a classic overfitting signature: a momentum ranking system with a 126-day time-stop, no skip period, and single-vintage entries has many degrees of freedom (formation window, ranking universe, sector mix) that can fit in-sample noise without capturing a repeatable cross-sectional effect.

The return concentration corroborates this fragility: monthly returns are dominated by a handful of outsized months (Dec-2023 +70%, Aug-2026 +57%, several +40% months) sitting alongside frequent double-digit negative months (2025 alone gave back -55.6% annually). A strategy with genuine cross-sectional edge shouldn't need a small number of "jackpot" months to carry the whole track record. P&L is also sector-concentrated — Capital Goods (+148k) and Financial Services (+88k) account for the bulk of profit, while Chemicals (-22.6k), Metals & Mining (-16.6k) and Realty (-13.2k) bled steadily — suggesting the model rode a few sector-momentum waves rather than a diversified, structural effect. Regime split (bullish 213k vs bearish 91k) shows it only worked when Nifty was already trending, consistent with momentum being regime-dependent rather than robust.

Follow-up ideas: (1) test the paper's actual overlapping-portfolio (multi-vintage) construction to see if smoothing removes the OOS collapse — single-vintage variance may be masking a real but weaker signal; (2) reintroduce the skip period and re-run OOS specifically on 2025 data, where reversal contamination looks worst.
