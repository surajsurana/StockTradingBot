# Analysis: Overnight Return Anomaly — PASS

The pass rests on a modest but genuine edge: +10.09 expectancy overall, +7.71 in holdout, positive across all walk-forward windows, with a small profit factor (1.11) and thin exposure (3.5%) consistent with a real but weak signal rather than overfitting. Given the implementation gap disclosed, this is notable — the actual execution (Open(t+1)→Open(t+2)) captures a full intraday session expected to fight *against* the overnight-persistence effect, meaning the true overnight-only signal is likely stronger than what's measured here. The strategy passing despite a structural headwind argues for a robust underlying mechanism, not a fragile one.

Performance was concentrated, not broad-based. P&L depended heavily on FMCG (+1665) and Chemicals (+1333), with Consumer Services marginally positive; Capital Goods (-405), Realty (-618), and Construction Materials (-736) were net drags — suggesting the anomaly is sector-conditional, likely tied to differences in overnight information flow or retail/institutional participation mix across these names. Regime dependence is stark: bearish-Nifty entries drove +2555 while bullish entries lost -991, implying the edge is asymmetric and concentrated in down-trending tape, possibly reflecting overnight fear/gap-fade dynamics rather than a symmetric anomaly. Monthly returns show clustering (strong Nov'24-Dec'24 losses, then recovery through mid-2025) rather than steady decay, consistent with regime-driven rather than time-decayed alpha.

Follow-ups: (1) test a session-aware execution model to isolate true overnight-only return, since current results are a likely-understated lower bound; (2) restrict to bearish-regime entries and FMCG/Chemicals-type sectors to test if edge concentration is stable out-of-sample or coincidental.
