**Why the verdict is REJECT — and why the sample is uninformative rather than merely "unlucky":**

With only 2 trades in ~2 years (exposure 3.63%, avg hold 27 days), this backtest cannot speak to the low-beta anomaly at all — it's two isolated data points, not a strategy track record. Both trades lost money (Financial Services -953.38, Power -969.18), producing a -961.28 expectancy and -1.92% max drawdown on capital. Notably, one loss occurred in a "bullish" Nifty regime and one in "bearish" — with n=2 this tells us nothing about regime dependency, it's simply too small to distinguish signal from noise. The rolling-annual view shows 11 months of flat 0.0% (no trade active) before both losses land in the same overlapping window, consistent with a single-vintage, low-turnover selection process rather than any repeated edge being tested.

Mechanically, trade scarcity is expected: this backtest only captures the long, unlevered, low-beta stock-selection leg (disclosed scope reduction) — the paper's actual claim requires a levered, beta-neutral long/short pairing with the high-beta short leg, which isn't implementable here (no NSE SLB, no margin). Combined with the 1-year (vs. paper's 5-year) volatility/correlation lookback — a disclosed deviation forced by the platform's frozen 3-year recency gate — beta estimates are noisier and the eligible-stock universe passing low-beta screens in any given window is apparently very thin, hence just 2 trades.

**Follow-ups regardless of verdict:** (1) Relax the single-vintage constraint to overlapping monthly rebalancing to generate more independent trade observations from the same underlying signal. (2) Test whether the low-beta screen threshold is too strict for NSE's cap-weighted universe under a 1-year window, producing near-empty eligible sets — widening the beta percentile bands may be needed to get a testable sample size before any verdict beyond REJECT is possible.
