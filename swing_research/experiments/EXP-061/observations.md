# Analysis: Cross-Sectional Momentum — REJECT

**Why the verdict holds:** The overall book shows a thin positive expectancy (+35.92/trade) and marginal profit factor (1.06), but this is entirely an in-sample/walk-forward artifact. On genuinely unseen data the strategy loses -228.98/trade — a swing large enough that the small aggregate edge cannot be trusted as real signal. With only 112 trades and a 22.3% win rate, the strategy is already a low-frequency, high-variance "few big winners carry many small losers" structure; single-vintage holding (one entry path per episode, no overlapping-portfolio smoothing) amplifies this variance exactly as it did for 52-Week High Momentum. A handful of favorable episodes in the training window likely drove the in-sample numbers, and that concentration didn't repeat out-of-sample.

**Dependencies:** Performance was concentrated in Power, Healthcare, and Realty (all >$6.9k), while Financial Services (-$8.6k) and Capital Goods (-$8.4k) alone erased most of the gains — momentum clearly broke down in the largest, most liquid sectors. Regime-wise, the strategy only worked in bearish-entry conditions (+$10.2k) and lost money on bullish entries (-$6.2k), the opposite of what a long-only momentum mechanism should show — a red flag consistent with the OOS breakdown rather than genuine regime-dependent alpha.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test overlapping multi-vintage construction to see if variance/OOS instability is a position-rule artifact rather than a signal problem. (2) Exclude or separately model Financial Services/Capital Goods, and investigate why bullish-regime entries underperform, before considering any resubmission.
