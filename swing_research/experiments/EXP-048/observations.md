# Analysis: Turn-of-the-Month Effect (PASS)

**Mechanism and why it likely worked:** The strategy captures a well-documented calendar anomaly — abnormal returns clustering around month-end/month-start — applied here at the individual-stock level via a 3-trading-day hold. The positive expectancy (+103.73/trade overall, +72.77/trade out-of-sample) and modest profit factor (1.51) suggest a real, if thin, edge consistent with liquidity/rebalancing flows (institutional month-end positioning, SIP/salary-driven retail inflows in the NSE context) rather than noise, especially given it held up across a genuine walk-forward split.

**Regime and sector dependence:** Returns are heavily regime-skewed — bullish-day entries generated 72,759 of the ~100,518 total P&L (72%) vs. 27,759 in bearish regimes, indicating the effect is a bull-market amplifier more than a standalone edge; note bearish-day trades were still net positive, so it isn't purely beta. Sector P&L is concentrated: Financial Services, Capital Goods, Consumer Durables and Auto/Auto Components together account for the large majority of profit, while Metals & Mining, Telecom, Diversified and Power were net losers — plausibly sectors less tied to retail/institutional month-end cash flows. The 2018 and 2024 annual returns (-21.96%, -12.03%) show the effect can reverse hard in adverse macro years, and the eligibility mechanism (per-month combined-rank, 40 slots) means sector coverage, not fundamentals, partly drives which names get sampled.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test an index-level (not per-symbol) implementation to match the original paper's actual claim. (2) Split expectancy by bull/bear regime and by sector explicitly to see if the edge is really "turn-of-month" or largely "turn-of-month-in-financials-during-uptrends."
