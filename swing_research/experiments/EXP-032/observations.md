# Analysis: Betting Against Beta — REJECT

**Why the numbers look this way.** The headline in-sample/overall figures (expectancy +110.1, PF 1.21, CAGR 7.13%) look mediocre-but-tolerable, yet the auditor's decisive check — out-of-sample expectancy of -15.19/trade — flips the verdict. This is consistent with the mechanism being tested here: we only capture the long, unlevered low-beta stock-selection leg, not the paper's leverage-scaled, beta-neutral long-short spread. A one-sided, unlevered low-beta book has much weaker structural edge than the documented factor, so it's unsurprising the edge doesn't survive genuinely unseen data even though it looked fine walk-forward. Compounding this, the 1-year (vs. paper's 3–5 year) volatility/correlation lookback makes beta estimates noisier and more regime-reactive, which plausibly degrades stock selection precisely in the out-of-sample window.

**Dependencies.** Returns were almost entirely a 2021 phenomenon (+33.3% in 2021 vs -10.4% in 2022), and concentrated in a bullish regime (+27,650 bullish vs -4,750 bearish) and one sector (Telecommunication +23,166, offsetting Oil Gas & Consumable Fuels -10,538 and Consumer Durables -3,457). A long-only low-beta anomaly leaning this hard on a single bull-market year and one sector is fragile, not diversified — exactly the profile that fails to replicate out-of-sample.

**Follow-ups.** (1) Test whether the edge is really sector-driven (ex-Telecom) rather than a genuine beta effect. (2) Once short/leverage infrastructure exists, re-test the true beta-neutral long-short spread rather than the long-only proxy, which this result cannot speak to.
