# Analysis: Amihud Illiquidity Premium — REJECT

The verdict rests entirely on sample size, not on the shape of the numbers themselves. With only 3 trades in the overall summary (the auditor's "4 total across all windows" includes an additional out-of-sample trade), the impressive-looking profit factor (5.24), CAGR (0.63%), and recovery factor (4.2) are statistical noise dressed as signal — any of these figures could flip entirely with one more losing trade. This is a direct consequence of the top-decile (≥90th percentile) illiquidity filter stacked on a 252-day formation window and single-vintage holding: by construction, very few names in the NSE universe qualify as "most illiquid" *and* survive whatever entry logic triggers a trade, so the strategy essentially becomes a 3-stock case study, not a portfolio test.

The sector concentration confirms this fragility: Capital Goods (+1326) and Oil & Gas (+1011) each represent a single trade carrying the entire result, while Financial Services (-446) is the lone detractor. There is no time-of-day data (P&L by entry-hour is empty — consistent with next-day-open fill timing under the execution-realism engine), and the bullish/bearish split (1326 vs 565) is just the same handful of trades relabeled, not independent evidence of regime dependence.

Two follow-up ideas regardless of verdict: (1) relax the decile threshold (e.g., top quintile) or lengthen the study period to generate enough trades to actually test the illiquidity premium, since the current design is starved by its own selectivity; (2) given execution-realism costs are expected to bite hardest here (Amihud selects directly on illiquidity), rerun with the ADV cap loosened to see how much of the raw edge survives slippage before dismissing the mechanism itself.
