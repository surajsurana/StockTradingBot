**Why REJECT despite decent headline metrics:**

The overall backtest (n=234, expectancy +57.6, PF 1.14, Sharpe 0.51) looks marginally profitable, but this blends in-sample/walk-forward data with a genuinely held-out out-of-sample slice where expectancy flips to -47.29/trade. That sign flip on unseen data is the single most damning signal available — an anomaly that only works on the data used to find/tune it is not a real edge, it's overfitting to sector concentration and regime luck. The Auditor is right to weight this over the aggregate numbers, which are inflated by in-sample fitting.

**Structural dependencies visible in the data:** P&L is heavily concentrated in a handful of sectors — Healthcare, Metals & Mining, Diversified, Textiles account for ~$25k of the $13.5k net (implying broad losses elsewhere: Telecom -4136, Capital Goods -5575, IT -2791). This is not a broad, robust liquidity effect; it's a few sectoral bets carrying the book while ~9 sectors bleed money. Similarly, regime dependency is stark: bullish-day entries generated +18,498 vs bearish -5,021 — the "anomaly" is really just long-only beta exposure dressed up as a turnover signal, consistent with the disclosed long-only constraint and the 2025 vs 2026 annual swing (+23.2% then -8.6%).

**Follow-up ideas:** (1) Re-run with sector-neutral construction or sector caps to test if the turnover signal survives outside Healthcare/Metals, since current PnL is sector-concentration, not factor breadth. (2) Explicitly split OOS performance by market regime — if OOS bearish periods explain most of the -47.29 expectancy, the real finding is regime fragility, not turnover invalidity, and a regime filter could be the next hypothesis worth testing standalone.
