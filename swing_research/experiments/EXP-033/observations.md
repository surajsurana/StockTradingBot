# Performance Analysis: Betting Against Beta (Low-Beta Anomaly) — REJECT

**Why the verdict stands despite decent aggregate numbers:** The full-sample metrics (Sharpe 0.91, expectancy +129.42, PF 1.30) look respectable, but these are dominated by in-sample/walk-forward periods. The auditor's -8.90 out-of-sample expectancy is the disqualifying number, and the monthly return pattern explains why: strong, broad-based gains cluster in 2024 (annual +30.2%), then the edge deteriorates sharply — 2025 is essentially flat (+0.03%) with five losing months including two >6% drawdowns (Oct -6.6%, Feb -7.5%), and 2026 opens with a -12.96% March before a partial April/May recovery. This is a classic in-sample-good/out-of-sample-fails pattern, exactly what the auditor flagged.

**Mechanism and dependencies:** This backtest only captures the long, unlevered low-beta stock-selection leg — not the paper's levered, beta-neutral long-short spread — so it was structurally never going to reproduce the paper's documented magnitude, only a diluted, one-sided fraction of it. Returns are almost entirely regime-dependent: bullish-day entries generated $34,024 of the $34,944 total P&L (97%), with bearish-day entries contributing almost nothing ($920). This is not a beta-hedged, market-neutral return stream — it's a long-only equity strategy that happens to work when Nifty is rising and stalls or loses money otherwise, consistent with 2025's choppier, more range-bound tape versus 2024's stronger uptrend. Sector concentration reinforces this fragility: Healthcare (+$13,240) and Automobiles (+$12,588) alone account for more than the entire net P&L, while Financial Services (-$7,444), Chemicals (-$2,455), and Services (-$2,249) bled steadily — a narrow, sector-idiosyncratic engine rather than a diversified factor premium.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test true beta-neutral construction (long low-beta / short high-beta, if/when SLB infra exists) to see if the paper's actual claim — not this diluted proxy — holds out-of-sample. (2) Investigate whether the 1-year vol/correlation lookback (vs. paper's 5-year) is injecting noise that causes late-sample beta misclassification, given the OOS breakdown coincides with the shortened-window regime.
