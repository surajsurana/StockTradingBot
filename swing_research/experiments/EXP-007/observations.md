# Analysis: Minervini Trend Template Filter — REJECT

The auditor's rejection is a **sample-size verdict, not a performance verdict** — note the apparent contradiction: the metrics table shows 70 trades with a 2.91 profit factor and 17.46% CAGR, yet the rejection cites "0 total trades" and "0 in holdout." This tells us the 70 trades shown are pooled/aggregate figures that failed to survive the walk-forward window structure — likely because the state-transition qualification proxy for VCP breakout produces so few genuine signals per rolling window that most windows (and the entire holdout) simply never fire. This is consistent with the disclosed caveat that our entry trigger is a raw template-qualification day rather than a true VCP pivot breakout — a rarer, more precisely-timed event — so signal scarcity across the multi-year holdout is expected, not anomalous.

The sector and regime concentration reinforces this fragility: P&L is dominated by Healthcare (+19.3k) and Capital Goods (+10.4k), with Automobile (-3.2k) and Chemicals (-1.8k) dragging negative — a handful of sector bets, not a diversified edge. The bullish/bearish split (18.5k vs 19.3k) shows no regime dependency, but with so few underlying trades this is uninformative rather than reassuring.

**Follow-ups**: (1) Loosen the entry trigger toward an actual pivot-breakout definition (tighter price/volume contraction + breakout confirmation) to generate enough signals per window to even test the hypothesis. (2) Test IBD-style RS ranking against index peers rather than the open 3/6/9/12-month blend, since criterion 8 may be admitting/excluding a materially different symbol universe — a likely contributor to signal scarcity.
