# Analysis: Turn-of-Month Effect (PASS)

**Why it likely worked:** The turn-of-month effect earned a modest but consistent edge (expectancy +55.8/trade, PF 1.38) over a very short 3-day holding window (avg 4.8 days), consistent with the well-documented mechanism of month-end institutional flows/rebalancing lifting equities briefly. The 100% positive walk-forward windows and positive OOS holdout (+100.53/trade over 106 trades) suggest this is a genuine, recurring calendar pattern rather than a curve-fit artifact — notable given it's being tested per-symbol on NSE stocks rather than the index-level return the original paper studied, so this result is really evidence the effect propagates into individual names, not a replication of the original claim.

**Dependencies:** Performance is concentrated in Financial Services, Healthcare, Construction Materials, and IT (all >2,400 in P&L), while Capital Goods (-2,602) and Consumer Services (-1,346) dragged results — suggesting sector-specific sensitivity to month-end flows (e.g., index-heavy/liquid sectors benefiting more from rebalancing flows than cyclicals). The regime split is notable: bearish-day entries produced *more* P&L (10,903) than bullish (5,948), hinting the effect may be partly a mean-reversion/liquidity-provision phenomenon rather than pure momentum. Annual returns were uneven (2024 was negative overall despite the pass), so the edge is not stable year-to-year, even though monthly-window expectancy held up.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test whether excluding Capital Goods/Consumer Services materially changes CAGR/Sharpe — sector exclusion as a filter. (2) Given the bearish-day outperformance, investigate whether the effect is better framed as "month-end liquidity reversal" and test entries conditioned explicitly on prior-day drawdowns rather than calendar date alone.
