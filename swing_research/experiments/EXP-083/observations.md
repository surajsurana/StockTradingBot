# Analysis: Crypto Trend Timing (Faber 10-month SMA) — REJECT

**Mechanism and why the headline numbers mislead:** The full-sample metrics (expectancy +260.35, profit factor 3.14, CAGR 30.4%) are dominated by a handful of extreme months — 2022-01 (+391%), 2025-02 (+599%), 2021-12 (+73%), 2025-11 (+96%) — almost certainly SOL/BTC/ETH catching the tail of major bull legs after the 10-month SMA flipped long. This is exactly the kind of long-tailed, low-hit-rate distribution (win rate 36.8%) that trend-following is supposed to produce: a few huge winners funding many small losers. The problem is that these outsized winners are concentrated in the *earlier*, in-sample/walk-forward period. On genuinely out-of-sample trades, expectancy collapses to -13.45/trade — the strategy is giving back its edge, not compounding it. Given the disclosed 20% one-directional stop, the 4% risk-per-unit sleeve is likely ejecting positions Faber's model would have held through the volatile mid-trend drawdowns (e.g., the 2023 bear chop: -30% to -27% across May/June/Aug/Sep/Oct, and 2024-10 and 2025-10 repeats), converting what should be "held through noise, exit on trend break" into "stopped out before the trend resumes." Combined with 0.30%/side + spread costs and the 31.2% no-offset VDA tax on every winner (with no relief on losers), the asymmetry needed for trend-following to survive is structurally eroded exactly on new data.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test wider/ATR-based stops or no hard stop (rely solely on SMA cross-under) to isolate how much of the OOS decay is stop-induced vs. genuine alpha decay. (2) Break OOS trades down by coin/regime (bull vs. chop) to see if losses are concentrated in the 2023/2025 sideways-bear regime, which would support waiting for higher regime-detection quality before resurrecting this idea.
