**Mechanism and why it likely worked:** The strategy longs top-decile (most illiquid, ILLIQ ≥ 90th percentile) NSE names, which is precisely the population where the execution-realism engine's slippage/ADV-cap costs bite hardest — yet the strategy still cleared the bar. That the *cost-adjusted* verdict (56 trades, 100% positive-expectancy walk-forward windows, +429.21/trade holdout) held up despite an illiquidity-linked slippage penalty that was expected to be "at least as large, plausibly larger" than in prior strategies is the strongest evidence the premium itself is real and not an artifact of a zero-cost backtest. Modest exposure (3.75%) and long average holds (21.8 days) are consistent with a slow-moving, fundamentals-driven illiquidity premium rather than a microstructure fluke.

No entry-hour data exists (single-vintage daily holding), so intraday timing can't be assessed.
