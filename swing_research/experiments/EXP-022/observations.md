# Analysis: Short-Term Reversal (PASS)

The mechanism — buying names that just fell sharply, exiting on a 21-day time-stop or 8% protective stop — earned its PASS on the auditor's core criteria: 100% of walk-forward windows positive, and a clean +151.55/trade holdout expectancy across 280 trades, consistent with the full-sample 465.5 expectancy and 1.43 profit factor. The win rate (45.9%) below 50% but profit factor above 1 confirms this is a classic asymmetric-payoff reversal profile: losers are cut (time-stop/8% stop) while winning reversals are allowed to run within the 21-day window.

Regime dependence is visible in the P&L split: bullish-day entries contributed 261k vs. 125k bearish — reversal buys work better when the broader tape offers a recovering tailwind rather than fighting a falling market, sensible since a single-name bounce is easier to realize inside 21 days when the index isn't also grinding down. Sector-wise, gains concentrated in Financial Services, Capital Goods, and Realty (high-beta, liquid, mean-reverting cyclicals), while FMCG (-21.3k) and Power (-11.3k) detracted — likely because low-beta/defensive or structurally trending-down names don't mean-revert on a 1-month horizon. Drawdown (47%) and monthly volatility (e.g., -47.4% Mar-2026, -31.6% Mar-2020) reflect the price paid for buying into weakness with no percentile-based early exit and a much shorter, higher-turnover holding period than the lab's momentum strategies.

Follow-ups: (1) test regime-conditioning the signal (bullish-only entries) given the 2:1 bullish/bearish P&L skew; (2) sector-filter out FMCG/Power/Telecom to isolate the cyclical-reversal edge before any capital allocation decision.
