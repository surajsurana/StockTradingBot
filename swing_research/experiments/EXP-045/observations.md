# Analysis: Idiosyncratic Volatility Anomaly (PASS)

The verdict rests on solid breadth: 1,146 trades, 100% of walk-forward windows positive, and a holdout expectancy (+146.63) that actually exceeds the full-sample expectancy (+125.89), suggesting no overfitting decay. But the quality of the edge is uneven and the risk-adjusted numbers are middling (Sharpe 0.65, Sortino 0.70, max drawdown 46.14%, profit factor 1.21) — consistent with a long-only, low-idio-vol selection leg with no source-paper risk overlay riding on top of a genuinely volatile stock universe rather than a clean factor return.

Sector P&L is highly concentrated: Capital Goods, Auto, Healthcare and FMCG carry the book (~$120k combined), while Consumer Services (-$30.3k), Consumer Durables (-$19k) and Realty (-$8.1k) bleed steadily — plausibly sectors where low realized idio-vol post-CAPM-residual is a poor proxy once SMB/HML tilts (size/value) are absent from the residual, letting some cyclical-discretionary names slip through as "low risk" when they aren't. Regime split (bullish $84.8k vs bearish $59.0k) shows the strategy isn't purely a bull-market artifact, which supports the PASS, though the annual/monthly returns (-19.5% in 2018, -32.6% in March 2020, -23.1% in March 2026) show it still gets hurt in sharp drawdowns despite being framed as "low volatility."
