# Analysis: Volume-Backed Breakout — REJECT

**Why it failed:** The core issue is a mechanism breakdown between the informal prototype (37.1% win rate, net positive over 5 years/26 symbols) and this walk-forward test (27.68% win rate, PF 0.72, expectancy -136.45). A ~9.4pp drop in win rate on a breakout system is consistent with the pattern's volume/price trigger identifying more false breakouts out-of-sample than in the original curated sample — exactly the kind of overfitting a first walk-forward test is designed to expose. With only 3.15% exposure and 10.5-day average holds, the strategy wasn't taking large concentrated bets; the erosion is broad-based and systematic, not a few blown-up trades.

**Sector/regime dependence:** P&L is heavily sector-skewed — Information Technology (-9092), Chemicals (-6562), Financial Services (-6487), and Services (-6197) account for the bulk of losses, while only Metals & Mining, Consumer Services, and Consumer Durables were modestly profitable. This suggests the breakout logic doesn't generalize across sector volatility/volume regimes — it may be tuned to momentum characteristics absent in IT/Financials/Chemicals recently. Regime split shows losses in both bullish (-24,230) and bearish (-15,203) Nifty days, so this isn't simply a wrong-regime problem — the edge appears absent regardless of market direction, reinforcing that the pattern itself lacks discriminative power out-of-sample.

**Follow-up ideas:** (1) Retest the pattern restricted to Metals/Consumer sectors only, to see if the edge is sector-conditional rather than universal. (2) Re-examine the volume-threshold definition against realistic slippage/liquidity in the walk-forward universe, since the win-rate collapse suggests trigger quality degraded, not just trade management.
