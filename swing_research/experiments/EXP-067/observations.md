**Why it failed:** The Amihud illiquidity premium bets that thinly-traded (top-decile ILLIQ) stocks earn a compensation premium for their illiquidity risk. With only 49 trades over ~2 years and a single 0/1 walk-forward window, there simply wasn't enough sample density to validate this cross-sectionally-designed premium under a single-vintage holding scheme -- a known moderate-to-material fidelity gap for this program. More importantly, this is the first strategy scored under execution-realism accounting, and Amihud selects on illiquidity *directly*: the ILLIQ-derived slippage cost and 5% ADV sizing cap bite hardest precisely on the names this strategy is designed to hold. Profit factor sits at 0.98 -- roughly breakeven pre-cost -- so the realistic execution drag was enough to tip a marginal edge into a small loss (expectancy -₹11.25/trade, CAGR -18%).

**Dependencies:** PnL was regime-lopsided -- bearish Nifty days contributed +₹4,366 while bullish days cost -₹4,917, odd for a long-only book and suggestive of illiquidity exposure proxying for a defensive/mean-reversion factor rather than a clean premium. Sector-wise, gains concentrated in Capital Goods, Metals & Mining and FMCG, while Telecom, Auto and Chemicals bled over -₹3,600 each -- indicating idiosyncratic, sector-driven noise rather than a systematic illiquidity effect.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Re-test with a rolling monthly regression matching the original methodology instead of single-vintage holding, to isolate that fidelity gap's contribution. (2) Compare raw vs. execution-realism-adjusted trade sets directly to quantify exactly how much of the edge is cost-destroyed versus genuinely absent.
