# Analysis: Short-Term Reversal — PASS

**Mechanism and why it likely worked:** This is a long-only, single-vintage reversal strategy buying recent losers on a 1-month formation window with a 21-day time-stop. The 46.2% win rate combined with a 1.37 profit factor and +510.64 expectancy is consistent with the classic reversal signature: more losing trades than winning ones, but winners meaningfully larger — plausible for a strategy that enters into short-term oversold conditions and captures a bounce, with the 8% stop capping the tail-risk trades where "buying the dip" becomes "catching a falling knife" (a real structural concern given entries follow sharp declines, unlike momentum entries).

**Dependencies:** P&L is heavily concentrated in Financial Services, Capital Goods, and Healthcare (~$390K combined, >60% of total PnL), while Chemicals, Power, and Services were net negative — suggesting the reversal effect is sector-conditional, likely tied to liquidity/mean-reverting microstructure in large-cap-heavy sectors rather than a universal factor. Regime split (bullish $418K vs bearish $211K) shows the strategy earns roughly 2x more on bullish-tagged entry days, implying reversal bounces are stronger/faster in an uptrend and the drawdown episodes (2020-03 -57%, 2022-05 -39%, 2024-10 -40%) cluster around bearish/volatile regimes where "losers" kept losing.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test a regime filter that reduces or skips entries when Nifty is bearish, given the 2:1 PnL skew. (2) Investigate sector-conditioning (e.g., restrict to Financial Services/Capital Goods/Healthcare) or diagnose why Chemicals/Power are structurally negative before generalizing this factor further.
