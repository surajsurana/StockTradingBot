# Analysis: Betting Against Beta — PASS

The PASS verdict (positive expectancy in 100% of walk-forward windows, +207.53/trade OOS) is statistically clean, but the mechanism being validated is narrower than the paper's headline claim. This backtest captures only the **long, unlevered, low-beta stock-selection leg** — not the leverage-scaled, beta-neutral long-short spread the original BAB paper documents. That the strategy still clears expectancy hurdles on this reduced construction is a meaningful result in its own right: low-beta NSE names generated a real edge (PF 1.32, CAGR 10.77%) without any leverage assist.

The P&L concentration confirms a plausible defensive-quality mechanism rather than noise: Healthcare (+35.9k), Auto (+33.7k), Consumer Services (+27.9k) and Telecom (+26.2k) — classically lower-beta, steady-cashflow sectors — drove the bulk of profits, while Oil & Gas (-15.5k) and Construction Materials (-4.0k), higher-beta cyclical/commodity-sensitive sectors, were the main drags. This sector split is consistent with a genuine low-beta tilt rather than a data-mined artifact.

The regime split is more concerning: bullish-day entries earned +152k vs only +26k bearish. A low-beta long-only book should ideally show relative resilience in down markets, not near-total dependence on bullish entries — this is expected given no short leg exists to monetize the high-beta side, but it means the strategy is really "low-beta stocks in an up-trending tape," not a validated all-weather anomaly. The max drawdown (36.2%) and Sharpe (0.81) also reflect the cost of leaving beta un-hedged.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test performance conditioned on Nifty regime more granularly (e.g., trailing 3-month trend) to quantify how much of the edge is beta-tilted market exposure vs. genuine stock-selection alpha. (2) Given the 1-year vs. paper's 3–5 year lookback tradeoff, run a sensitivity check comparing 1-year vs. 2-year windows once more history accrues, to see if the OOS expectancy is stable or an artifact of the shortened, noisier beta estimate.
