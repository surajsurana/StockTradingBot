# Analysis: Turn-of-the-Month Effect — PASS

**Mechanism and why it likely worked:** The turn-of-month effect (institutional flow/rebalancing patterns around month boundaries) was tested here as a per-symbol signal rather than the paper's original index-level signal — a meaningful scope change. Every qualifying symbol on a given calendar day competes for the same shared engine cap (max_units_total=10), so the realized trade set depends heavily on the per-month combined-rank eligibility filter (top 40 by symbol×month hash) rather than any economic ranking. With a short, fixed 3-trading-day hold (row-position based, not calendar-approximated), the effect is capturing a narrow, mechanical post-entry drift rather than a sustained trend — consistent with the modest expectancy (+95.05 overall, +62.52 OOS) and thin Sharpe/Sortino (0.752/0.372) despite a healthy profit factor (1.45).

**Sectoral/regime dependence:** Healthcare (+18,978) and Financial Services (+17,772) drove most of the P&L, with Realty, Media, and IT contributing meaningfully; Capital Goods (-5,922), Telecom (-3,945), and Consumer Services (-2,401) were consistent drags — suggesting the effect is not universal across NSE sectors and may reflect sector-specific flow patterns rather than a market-wide calendar anomaly. Performance was fairly balanced across regimes (bullish +49,576 vs bearish +42,531), implying the edge isn't purely a beta-driven artifact of broad market direction, which is a mild positive for the "pure" turn-of-month thesis. The severe 2018 (-26.97%) and 2019 (-9.47%) drawdown years, however, suggest sensitivity to specific macro/liquidity regimes not captured by simple bull/bear tagging.

**Follow-up ideas:** (1) Re-run with symbols restricted to Healthcare/Financials-heavy universe to test if the edge is sector-concentrated or genuinely diffuse. (2) Compare this per-symbol result against an index-level replication of the original paper to isolate whether the effect is a market-wide phenomenon miscast at the stock level.
