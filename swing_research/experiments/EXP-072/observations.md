**Why REJECT is the right read of these numbers**

The overall backtest looks superficially attractive — expectancy +48.35, PF 1.12, CAGR 5.1%, positive total P&L of ~16.1k — but the auditor's decisive check is out-of-sample expectancy at -55.48/trade, which flips the sign entirely. That gap between headline and OOS numbers is exactly what you'd expect from a strategy built on a single-factor residual-vol proxy standing in for the paper's 3-factor construction: the single-factor version may capture a noisier, less stable slice of "idiosyncratic" risk that fits in-sample/walk-forward windows without generalizing. The regime and sector breakdowns reinforce fragility rather than robustness: essentially all profit (23,186 bullish vs -7,038 bearish) came from bullish Nifty days, and P&L concentration in Healthcare/Services/Financials against material bleed in Chemicals, Power, Consumer Services and Metals & Mining suggests the edge is a narrow, regime- and sector-dependent effect, not a broad cross-sectional anomaly. The declining annual returns (2023 +10%, 2024 +22%, 2025 -3%, 2026 -13%) and negative rolling-annual trend into 2025-26 corroborate genuine decay, consistent with an in-sample-fit-driven result rather than a persistent factor.
