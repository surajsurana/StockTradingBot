# Analysis: Short-Term Reversal (PASS)

**Mechanism fit and why it worked**: The strategy buys recent losers expecting a bounce within a ~24-day average holding window (vs. a 21-day time-stop), which is broadly consistent across the sample: 48.24% win rate but a 1.41 profit factor and +247.69 expectancy indicates winners are larger than losers — typical of reversal trades where the mean-reversion pop, when it happens, is sized well ahead of losses capped near the 8% stop. The heavy dependence on a **bullish Nifty regime** (₹68,887 of ₹91,398 total P&L, ~75%) is mechanistically sensible: buying oversold names into a strengthening tape gives the reversal more room to complete before the 21-day stop forces an exit, whereas oversold names in a falling market tend to keep falling — precisely the risk noted about entries starting closer to further downside than a momentum entry would.

**Sector dependence**: Gains concentrated in Healthcare, Capital Goods, Metals & Mining, and IT (~₹87k combined), while Oil & Gas, Consumer Durables, Construction Materials, and Consumer Services were net negative (~-₹24k combined) — consistent with reversal working better in liquid, news-driven sectors with mean-reverting flows and worse in sectors prone to sustained downtrends (commodity-linked, consumption-cyclical).

**Follow-ups**: (1) Test regime-conditioning explicitly — gate entries on Nifty trend/breadth given the 3:1 bullish/bearish P&L skew. (2) Given single-vintage vs. paper's overlapping-portfolio construction and the short 1-month holding period, run a portfolio-turnover/capacity analysis before combining with other cross-sectional strategies.
