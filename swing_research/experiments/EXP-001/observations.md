# Performance Analysis: Turtle System 2 (Long-Only, NSE Cash Equities)

The trend-following mechanism -- entering on 55-day Donchian breakouts and riding trends with wide stops -- produces the classic signature seen here: a low win rate (37.5%) offset by a profit factor above 1 (1.42), meaning the system depends on a small number of large winners to pay for frequent small losses. This is consistent with the long average holding period (47.2 days) and low market exposure (14.6%), both hallmarks of a strategy that sits out most of the time waiting for the rare sustained breakout.

The regime data shows the strategy captured trends in both bullish and bearish Nifty tapes (bearish entries actually contributed more, ₹9,120 vs ₹7,581 bullish), suggesting the breakout logic is direction-agnostic rather than dependent on a rising market -- reasonable for a long-only trend system riding sector-specific moves rather than broad index beta. Sector concentration is stark: Oil & Gas (₹18,855) and Consumer Services (₹17,359) drove essentially all the profit, while Healthcare and IT each cost over ₹7,500. This is a classic single-strategy problem -- with only one asset class and no diversification across the ~20+ uncorrelated futures markets the original Turtle system relied on, a couple of strong sector trends are propping up the whole equity curve, which explains the shallow Sharpe (0.43) despite a positive expectancy.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test sector-conditioned entry filters or exclude chronically choppy sectors (IT, Healthcare) to see if expectancy improves out-of-sample. (2) Given the reliance on 2-3 sectors, extend the study to more NSE sectors/indices to check whether diversification breadth (not just time) is what's missing before drawing conclusions about long-term breakout transferability to single-market equity trading.
