# Analysis: Short-Term Reversal — PASS

The mechanism here is buying recent losers and expecting a bounce within a ~1-month window (21-day time-stop). The numbers are consistent with a real but weak edge: a low 39.1% win rate offset by winners that are large enough to produce a positive expectancy (+47.91/trade) and profit factor just above breakeven (1.115). This is the classic reversal signature — most mean-reversion bets fail to bounce and get stopped out or time-stopped flat/negative, but the minority that do revert produce outsized gains, which is exactly what a positive-skew, low-hit-rate expectancy profile looks like.

The regime breakdown is the most telling dependency: PnL is heavily concentrated in bearish-Nifty entries (+3,715) while bullish-day entries are net negative (-410). This makes mechanistic sense — reversal candidates (sharp recent decliners) are more plentiful and more likely to genuinely mean-revert during broad-market weakness, whereas in bullish tape a beaten-down name may simply be leaking a real deterioration rather than a bounce candidate. Sector-wise, the edge is concentrated in Capital Goods (+6,076) and Chemicals (+2,484), while Consumer Services (-4,295) and Realty (-2,543) are net drags — plausibly higher-beta, sentiment-driven sectors where "falling knife" names kept falling past the 8% stop rather than reverting.

Given the long-only constraint and the paper's short leg being unavailable, a natural follow-up is testing whether the bearish-regime dependency is strong enough to warrant regime-gating entries, and whether excluding Realty/Consumer Services (or sizing them down) materially improves the profile without curve-fitting.
