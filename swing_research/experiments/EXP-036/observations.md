# Performance Analysis: Cross-Sectional Momentum (PASS)

**Why it likely worked:** This is a long-only, single-vintage J=6/K=6 momentum system with no skip period and only a time-stop/8% stop exit. The low win rate (30%) paired with a strong profit factor (2.05) and expectancy (+975.56) is the classic momentum signature — most positions are small losers or time-stopped flats, while a minority of winners (trending capital-goods/healthcare/power names) run far enough to dominate P&L. The auditor's pass is corroborated by regime data: bearish-Nifty entries actually produced *more* P&L (68,668) than bullish entries (38,643), suggesting the strategy is picking up idiosyncratic relative-strength continuation rather than simply riding beta — consistent with genuine cross-sectional ranking rather than a disguised market-timing bet.

**Sector dependency:** Performance is concentrated — Capital Goods (54,371) alone is roughly half of total P&L, with Healthcare and Power adding another ~40k. Conversely, Metals & Mining (-8,799), Automobile (-7,841), and Oil & Gas (-5,016) were consistent drags, likely cyclical/commodity-sensitive names whose momentum reversed sharply within the 126-day holding window (no skip period may have let short-term reversal noise contaminate entries here specifically).

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test sector-neutral or sector-capped position sizing, since Capital Goods concentration risk isn't visible in the aggregate Sharpe/drawdown figures. (2) Add the paper's skip-period refinement specifically for cyclical/commodity sectors (Metals, Auto, Oil&Gas) to test whether reversal contamination — not momentum failure — explains their negative P&L.
