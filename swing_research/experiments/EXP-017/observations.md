**Why it passed, but weakly**

The strategy clears the bar (positive expectancy overall, at holdout, and in half the walk-forward windows) but the underlying return stream is thin and lumpy. Win rate is only 26.85%, so the positive expectancy of ₹157/trade is being carried by a profit factor of 1.28 — i.e., a small number of large winners offsetting frequent small losses, consistent with a long-only momentum book where most "qualifying episodes" chop sideways or get stopped out at the 8% protective stop (not part of the original paper) while a minority run to the 126-day time-stop with large gains. Sharpe (0.33) and recovery factor (0.66) confirm the equity curve is unstable relative to its 21.9% drawdown, and the 2024 (-8.3%) → 2025 (-0.4%) → 2026 (+25.7%) annual split shows the entire result is rescued by one outsized period (Aug-2026: +29.7% monthly), not steady factor capture.

The regime split is the clearest diagnostic: -₹23,057 in bullish-entry trades vs +₹40,022 in bearish-entry trades. That's counter to textbook momentum (which usually feeds on trend continuation in up markets) and instead suggests the single-vintage, no-skip-period construction is catching post-selloff reversals/relief rallies in beaten-down winners rather than genuine cross-sectional momentum persistence. Sector P&L reinforces concentration risk: Auto, Telecom and Capital Goods alone contribute ~₹32,700, while Financial Services, FMCG and Services bleed a combined ~-₹12,800 — the "momentum" edge is really 3 sectors carrying 13.
