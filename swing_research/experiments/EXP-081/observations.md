# Analysis: Earnings Announcement Premium (PASS)

The mechanism — buying expected-announcer stocks ahead of scheduled earnings — earned a modest edge (49.4% win rate, PF 1.34, expectancy ₹168.57) that survived walk-forward and holdout testing, but the equity curve tells a regime-dependent story. The market-regime split is stark: +₹63,871 on bullish Nifty entry days vs. -₹5,882 on bearish days. This is consistent with a long-only, concentrated (max 10 positions), equal-risk-sized book — with no short leg to hedge market beta, the "premium" is largely riding beta plus a volatility-timing effect around earnings, not a clean idiosyncratic announcement effect. The 2023 annual return (+44.1%) coincided with a strong bull tape, while 2025 (-15.2%) coincided with broad weakness — the rolling annual return series shows the strategy's fortunes tracking macro regime almost one-for-one, cresting near +61% mid-2024 and cratering to -23% in late 2025.

Sectorally, Financial Services, Capital Goods, and Healthcare drove nearly all profit (₹30.1k/₹16.0k/₹15.8k), while Information Technology (-₹7.8k), Metals & Mining, and Chemicals bled — likely because IT/Metals earnings months often coincide with global-cyclical or currency-driven drawdowns where the 8% stop fires disproportionately (stops are flagged as firing more in these volatile announcement windows).

Follow-ups: (1) test the effect market-neutral (long-short or beta-hedged) to isolate true announcement alpha from regime beta; (2) since volume-concentration ranking decides which ~10 of 150-250 announcers are held, run sensitivity/turnover analysis on that ranking rule and check if excluding IT/Metals materially changes the verdict.
