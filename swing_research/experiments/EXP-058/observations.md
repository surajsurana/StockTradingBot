# Analysis: Short-Term Reversal (PASS)

The mechanism — buying recent losers on a 1-month formation window and exiting via 21-day time-stop or 8% stop — earned its PASS on robustness (100% of walk-forward windows positive, strong out-of-sample expectancy of +529.92/trade) rather than on smooth, uniform performance. The equity curve tells a regime story: modest, choppy returns from 2016-2019, a sharp drawdown in March 2020 (-50.9%) consistent with reversal strategies getting run over in a crash-then-rebound liquidity event, followed by an increasingly explosive P&L regime from 2022-2026 with monthly swings exceeding ±100-200. This escalation likely reflects genuine mean-reversion edge strengthening in a higher-volatility, higher-turnover NSE tape — plausible given the strategy's short 22.9-day average hold and low 2.95% exposure, meaning a small number of concentrated, high-conviction reversal trades are driving outsized contribution once they hit.

Sector dependence is concentrated: Capital Goods (+587k) and Metals & Mining (+253k) anchor the P&L, alongside Healthcare and Autos — sectors with high beta and frequent sharp pullbacks, exactly where reversal signals should fire. Consumer Services (-120k) and Oil Gas (-64k) detracted, suggesting the mechanism struggles in sectors with more persistent (trending) drawdowns rather than mean-reverting ones. The bullish/bearish split (1.29M vs 0.52M) shows the strategy works in both regimes but leans on bullish-day entries, consistent with buying dips within an uptrend rather than catching falling knives in bearish regimes.
