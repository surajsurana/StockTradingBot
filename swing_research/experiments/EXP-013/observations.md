# Analysis: 52-Week High Momentum — PASS

**Why it likely worked:** The 52-week-high momentum effect is a well-documented persistence signal, and the numbers here are consistent with that mechanism operating cleanly on the long side. Win rate is low (35.95%) but profit factor is strong (2.30) — the classic trend-following signature of many small losses cut by the 8% stop and a smaller number of large winners that ride the full 126-day time-stop toward continuation. Expectancy of +2220/trade and the OOS holdout expectancy of +1576/trade point to a genuine, if noisy, edge rather than curve-fit residue — unsurprising given 100% of walk-forward windows were positive.

**Dependencies:** P&L is heavily concentrated in Financial Services (+252.9K) and Capital Goods (+145.1K), together dwarfing everything else; Metals & Mining and Realty are distant but real contributors. Telecommunication (-10.2K) and Textiles (-10.9K) were consistent drags, suggesting the 52-week-high signal doesn't travel well into low-liquidity or structurally range-bound sectors. Regime split is balanced (bullish +352.6K vs bearish +326.8K), so the edge isn't purely a bull-market artifact — encouraging given the single-vintage construction's higher path-dependency. The 2020-03 (-49.2%) and 2025-01/02/03 drawdown cluster show the strategy is vulnerable to sharp regime breaks mid-holding, consistent with having no early-exit rule.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Test the reserved percentile-based early-exit variant as a separately labeled iteration to see if it reduces the max-drawdown (30.8%) without eroding the time-stop's edge. (2) Given single-vintage variance concerns, rerun with the paper's overlapping K-vintage construction to check whether Financial Services/Capital Goods concentration and the 2020-03 tail loss are single-entry artifacts or genuine factor exposure.
