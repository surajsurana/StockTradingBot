**Why it passed, mechanistically:** The Amihud illiquidity premium bets that the most illiquid decile (top ILLIQ percentile ≥90) earns a compensating return for holding hard-to-trade names. That mechanism is directly in tension with the execution-realism engine now used for the verdict: ILLIQ-derived slippage is calibrated *from* illiquidity, so a strategy that selects on illiquidity by construction eats the largest slippage haircuts of any strategy in this program. That the strategy still cleared PASS (positive expectancy of 266.29/trade, PF 1.41, 50% of walk-forward windows positive, holdout +39.48/trade) after that headwind is the notable signal here — the raw zero-cost edge was almost certainly larger, and what survived is the post-cost residual.

**Dependencies:** Performance is heavily regime- and sector-concentrated. Bullish-Nifty entries drove +35,489 in P&L versus -1,937 in bearish regimes — this looks like a long-only illiquidity-premium harvest that only pays out when market beta is tailwind-positive, not a robust liquidity-risk premium standing on its own. Sector P&L is dominated by Capital Goods (+19,221) and Healthcare (+13,442), while Automobile/Auto Components (-9,374) and Telecommunication (-3,770) were consistent drags — consistent with illiquid small/mid-cap pockets in those two sectors behaving idiosyncratically rather than reflecting a clean cross-sectional premium. Annual returns swinging from -18.4% (2025) to +35.9% (2023) with only 3.28% exposure and low Sharpe (0.56)/Sortino (0.32) suggest a thin, timing-fragile edge rather than a persistent structural premium.
