The PASS verdict rests on thin but consistent evidence: only 65 overall trades (74 across walk-forward, 55 holdout), a low 33.85% win rate offset by a healthy 1.93 profit factor and +553 expectancy — classic momentum-factor signature where a minority of large winners (recovery factor 1.83, Sortino 3.25 vs Sharpe 0.71) carry many small losers. This is consistent with the 52-week-high mechanism: names breaking to new highs either continue strongly or stall/reverse quickly, and the 126-day time-stop (single-vintage, no early exit) lets winners run their full course while losers bleed for the same duration before being cut.

The regime split is the most telling number: -3,804 in bullish-entry trades vs +39,761 in bearish-entry trades. This suggests the edge is concentrated in buying strength when the broader tape is weak — a contrarian-to-index, momentum-in-name effect rather than a beta-driven one. Sector-wise, Auto/Auto Components (+16.2k) and Financial Services (+9.0k) dominate, while Capital Goods (-4.3k), FMCG (-3.7k) and IT (-2.8k) detract, hinting the effect is sector-idiosyncratic rather than universal.

Given the single-vintage structural deviation flagged, this result is one realized path, not the paper's smoothed multi-vintage average — variance here could be inflated either way.
