# Analysis: Betting Against Beta — REJECT

The verdict rests on one dominant fact: out-of-sample expectancy is -4.88/trade despite a rosy full-sample expectancy of +175.4 and rolling annual returns that peaked near +49% in mid-2024. This is a textbook in-sample/regime-decay pattern, not noise — the strategy's fortunes track the 2023-2024 bull run almost exactly (rolling annual return climbs from ~6% in Jan-2023 to ~49% by Jul-2024) and then deteriorates sharply from late-2024 onward (rolling annual return falls to -11.8% by Oct-2025), coinciding with sharp monthly drawdowns (Oct-24: -9.3%, Feb-25: -9.2%, Mar-26: -16.1%). The P&L-by-regime split (bullish 64,714 vs bearish 5,270) confirms the edge was never really "low-beta outperformance" in a risk-managed sense — it was a long-only, unlevered equity book that made money because Indian equities rallied, and low-beta names simply rode that rally with less drag. Once the tailwind faded, the walk-forward/OOS window exposed the strategy as having no genuine standalone edge. This is consistent with the disclosed scope gap: without the paper's leverage-scaled, beta-neutral construction, you're only capturing the long low-beta leg, which the paper itself predicts is the smaller, directionally-exposed half of the true factor — exactly the kind of construction that looks fine in a bull sample and fails once beta stops being a good proxy for "safety" in a genuinely new period. Sector data reinforces market-beta dependence rather than idiosyncratic stock-picking skill: gains concentrated in cyclical/consumer-discretionary sectors (Auto 21k, Consumer Services 11.9k, Financial Services 10.6k) that thrive in risk-on tapes, while defensive/rate-sensitive FMCG (-6.4k) and Chemicals (-3.2k) — sectors that should theoretically benefit from a true low-beta premium in a chop/down market — actually lost money. That is the opposite of what a working low-beta anomaly should show in a weak-regime OOS slice.

Follow-ups for a future hypothesis: (1) Test a genuine long-short or at least a long/cash market-neutral overlay (e.g., inverse-index futures proxy) once short/leverage infrastructure exists, to isolate the factor from beta-1 market direction — this result cannot distinguish "low beta works" from "long equities worked in 2023-24." (2) Re-run the 1-year lookback sensitivity explicitly against a 3-year window on a longer available history outside the frozen recency gate, to check whether the shortened vol/correlation window is amplifying the late-sample beta misclassification (i.e., stocks whose realized beta jumped after the 2024 rally may have been mis-sorted as "low beta" heading into the 2025 drawdown).
