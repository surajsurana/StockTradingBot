# Why REJECT: 52-Week High Momentum

The auditor's verdict rests entirely on out-of-sample expectancy (-58.68/trade) failing the single decisive test, despite the overall blended sample showing positive expectancy (313.18), a >1.6 profit factor, and a rising equity curve into 2026. This divergence is consistent with the mechanism: 52-week-high momentum is a low-frequency, fat-tailed factor (win rate only 36.8%, so most of the edge comes from a few large winners), and this implementation runs single-vintage holding rather than the paper's overlapping-portfolio construction. With only one entry point per qualifying episode instead of a smoothed multi-vintage average, results are highly path-dependent -- the in-sample/walk-forward period likely caught a favorable run of episodes (note the strong 2025-10 and 2026-07/08 months, +7.8% and +17.6%, which are doing most of the heavy lifting), while the OOS window apparently did not catch an equivalent tail winner and absorbed the base-rate losing trades instead. This is exactly the kind of variance the auditor's own MODERATE-to-MATERIAL vintage-structure caveat warns about.

Sector concentration reinforces the fragility: Healthcare (+18,466) and Capital Goods (+7,495) account for nearly all P&L, while seven other sectors are net negative (Automobile -3,699, Oil & Gas -2,001). A strategy this dependent on two sectors' idiosyncratic runs is unlikely to generalize OOS.

Follow-ups: (1) rerun with the paper's true overlapping multi-vintage construction to see if smoothing removes the OOS collapse; (2) test sector-neutral or ex-Healthcare/Capital-Goods variants to check if the edge is factor-driven or sector-driven.
