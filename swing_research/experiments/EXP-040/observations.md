# Analysis: Cross-Sectional Momentum — REJECT

**Why the verdict follows from the mechanism:** With only 69 trades across a single walk-forward window, the strategy never got the chance to demonstrate repeatability — the auditor's 0/1 positive-expectancy windows finding isn't about the sign of the number so much as the complete absence of a second data point to confirm it wasn't luck. This is compounded by the single-vintage holding construction: instead of the paper's overlapping-portfolio smoothing, each qualifying episode realizes one entry point's path. That mechanically inflates variance — visible in the 19.48% max drawdown against a mere 4.17% return on capital (recovery factor 0.205) and a Sharpe of 0.185 despite a "positive" expectancy of 60.38. The win rate of 30.4% with PF 1.11 shows this is a classic momentum shape (few big winners, many small losers) — but with only one window, we can't tell if the big winners are structural or survivorship of a lucky stretch (the 2026-08 monthly return of +19.72% single-handedly rescues an otherwise negative multi-year P&L, per the -5.38%/-8.32%/+17.87% annual sequence).

**Dependencies:** The strategy leaned entirely on bearish-regime entries (+$8,719) while bleeding in bullish regimes (-$4,553) — the opposite of what a long-only momentum book "should" want, suggesting mean-reversion or short-covering bounces drove entries rather than genuine trend continuation. Sector P&L is concentrated and lopsided: Healthcare/Chemicals/Power carried the book (+$14,374 combined) while Financial Services and IT alone erased over $6,500 — no diversified cross-sectional edge, just a few sector bets.

**Follow-ups:** (1) Extend the walk-forward to multiple J=6/K=6 windows before any re-test — one window is statistically meaningless regardless of the sign. (2) Test whether the bearish-regime dependency is a Nifty-beta artifact rather than idiosyncratic momentum, by conditioning entries on regime explicitly.
