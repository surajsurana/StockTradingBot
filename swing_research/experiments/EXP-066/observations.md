**Why REJECT despite decent-looking headline numbers**

The overall book shows barely-positive expectancy (₹6.33/trade) built on a profit factor of 1.017 — essentially a coin-flip with a razor-thin edge. That fragile edge is exactly what evaporates out-of-sample (-₹40.33/trade): with only 205 trades and 6.79% exposure, the strategy never had enough independent bets to prove the in-sample edge was real rather than noise, and a 1-year (vs. paper's 5-year) beta-estimation window makes the low-beta ranking itself noisier and more regime-reactive — precisely the kind of instability that shows up as walk-forward numbers looking fine while fresh data fails.

Mechanistically, this is only the long, unlevered stock-selection leg of a strategy the paper designed to be levered and beta-neutral; without the short leg's diversification and leverage, we're exposed to plain equity beta and sector concentration rather than a clean factor spread. That shows in the regime split: bullish-day entries made money (+3712), bearish-day entries lost (-2414) — the "low-beta" book still moves with the market, undermining the anomaly's core defensive premise. Sector P&L is similarly lopsided and idiosyncratic: Textiles/Healthcare (+5270/+4535) carried the book while FMCG, Services, Construction Materials, IT (-3035 to -1743) dragged it — concentration risk, not a broad low-beta effect.

**Follow-ups:** (1) test a true beta-neutral long/short (once SLB/margin exists) to see if the paper's actual spread survives on NSE; (2) run sensitivity of the ranking to 1yr vs 3yr beta windows to isolate how much of the OOS failure is estimation noise vs. a genuinely absent anomaly.
